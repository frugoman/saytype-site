Mac dictation stops by itself: why, and what to do

Updated November 9, 2026

You start Dictation on your Mac, pause to think, and the microphone icon is gone. This is the most common complaint about built-in Dictation. Here is what is going on, what is worth trying, and what to do if you want dictation that doesn't run on a session at all.

Why it stops

Apple's Dictation is a session: you start it with a shortcut or the menu, it listens, and it ends. Reports from users and several guides say it ends after roughly 30 seconds in which it hears no speech, and also when you click elsewhere, switch apps or change the input device. Apple doesn't document a setting that removes this, so treat the exact number as a user-reported behavior and not a promise.

Things to try first

  1. Turn Dictation off and on again in System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation, then test in Notes. If it works there, the problem is the app you were in.
  2. Check the microphone input. Under Sound → Input, make sure the right device is selected and its level moves when you talk. A headset that reconnects mid-session ends Dictation.
  3. Check Voice Control. If Voice Control is on, it takes over dictation.
  4. Keep the text field focused. Clicking another window or field ends the session.
  5. Restart the Mac if it fails everywhere, including Notes.

None of these removes the silence timeout. If you think while you talk, you will keep re-starting it.

A different model: no session to end

SayType is built around the opposite idea. There is no start button. The microphone is on only while a text field has keyboard focus, and your words are typed after a short pause (0.8 seconds by default, adjustable per app). A long silence doesn't end anything: you click out of the field and it stops listening, click back in and it listens again. Password fields are always skipped.

If you'd rather control it yourself, SayType also has push-to-talk (hold ⌃⌥Space, release to type) and toggle mode (press once to start, once to stop). In Settings you can set a longer pause for apps where you tend to think mid-sentence, such as Mail, and a shorter one for chat.

Speech is turned into text on your Mac with an on-device model (Whisper, or Parakeet v3 for 25 European languages), so there is no connection to lose. The model downloads once, about 630 MB.

Which should you use?

  • Short bursts, occasional use, nothing to install: built-in Dictation is fine, and a retry fixes most stops.
  • Long-form writing, thinking aloud, or dictating into Slack, Terminal or an editor all day: an always-ready or push-to-talk tool saves the constant restarting.

FAQ

Why does Mac dictation turn off by itself?

Built-in Dictation is a session. Users report it ends after about 30 seconds without speech, and it also ends if you switch apps, click another field or the microphone device changes.

Can I make Mac Dictation listen longer?

Apple doesn't document a setting that removes the silence timeout. Restarting it, or using an app with push-to-talk or an always-listening mode, are the practical options.

Does SayType stop listening after a pause?

No. SayType listens while a text field has focus and types your words after a short pause (0.8 seconds by default). It stops only when focus leaves a text field, or in push-to-talk and toggle modes when you release or toggle.
